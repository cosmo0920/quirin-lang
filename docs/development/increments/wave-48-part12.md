**GATE-SLOT-SHORT-JOB-LANE** (round 30; integrator's launch, 2026-11-08, on the user's decision "Yes, add a fast lane" to "Should a short job, like a 3-minute prepare, be allowed to jump ahead of long queued audits?"; discharges GATE-SLOT-SHORT-BUILD-WAIT registered by FLUENT-BIT-LINK-PREPARE; requirement R13 of docs/plans/gate-time-reduction/README.md, added by this card; [report](../../reports/gate-slot-short-job-lane/README.md)). The incident: `prepare clang-build --link` (3 min 17 s, peak 832 MB) waited 5 h 8 min for an audit slot at a 4 GiB budget behind audits and rust-suite drains. (1) scripts/gate_slot.py has a `short` class, the short-job lane: two slots (`<gate dir>/short/0,1`), its own arrival-order queue (family `short`), and no audit lock, so it is admitted ahead of queued audits and is not closed by a draining rust-suite job. Eligibility is enforced, not assumed: `--max-wall` (default 30 min, at most 1 h, cannot be switched off, `--deadline` refused) stops the command past it (SIGTERM, SIGKILL after a stop grace of at most 2 min; exit 124; receipt `lane.wall_overrun`, `lane.complete: false`) because a draining suite waits for lane jobs only as long as their bound; the exit is non-zero so no caller reads a stopped job as finished, and the lane does not clean up after the command (it must write its completion record last); `--memory-budget 1G|2G` is the memory the job declares (an excess is warned and recorded, a process tree over 4 GiB, the smallest audit budget, is stopped as `memory`). (2) Memory admission: a lane job is admitted when MemAvailable less the unspent declared memory of the lane jobs running covers its declaration; an audit takes the same reserve from its reading (declared budget less the resident set of the last sample, per held lane slot), so the lane counts against audits before it has allocated and not twice afterwards. (3) Drains: a rust-suite job drains the audits with the lane open; once they are drained it closes the lane (`short/admission`) and waits for the lane jobs in flight, which end within their wall bound plus a sample and the stop grace (bound a little over an hour, `SHORT_DRAIN_BOUND`), then holds both lane slots while its command runs so its load-sensitive rows run alone (a lane job arriving meanwhile waits for the suite); a longer wait gives up like an audit drain with the lane holder named. A lane job nested under a suite is covered by its slots. (4) Older clients (pre-queue and queue-only scripts): no `short` class, no lane lock, so no deadlock and no double admission of any slot; not guaranteed: an older rust-suite does not close the lane (a lane job may run beside it) and an older audit does not subtract the lane's reserve. `gate_slot.py status` lists lane holders and waiters (`short_lane` in --json) and a suite waiting for the lane. Tests: scripts/tests/test_gate_slot_lane.py (32: overtaking queued audits and a draining suite, the slot cap and lane order, memory admission and the audit reserve, suite and lane interaction with a wedged lane job and a real overrun, the command line and the receipt, both older scripts run against the current one in one scratch gate, kill -9 and dead or stopped waiters, status) in scratch gate directories; an incident replay in a scratch gate (docs/reports/gate-slot-short-job-lane/replay.json): the queue-only client ran the prepare last in 5 of 5 trials, this card ran it first, before the holder ended, in 5 of 5, with the queued jobs' order unchanged.
