27. The mutex model supports initially-unlocked non-recursive mutexes only.
    Dynamic shapes, condition variables, try/timed locks, rwlocks and recursive
    acquisition are represented refusals, not omissions. `remaining-t02`.
    **Amended, card `W47-CONDVAR-JOIN-PUBLICATION` (wave 47; user decision
    of 2026-09-28: lift only what the in_tail track needs).** Three condition
    variable shapes are lifted, and only on the census route of gateway rule
    10q, which derives them; nothing else about the mutex model changes. (1)
    `pthread_cond_wait` and `pthread_cond_timedwait` inside a `while`/`for`
    loop that rechecks a predicate, where the predicate and the guarded
    results are read under the same mutex the wait releases and reacquires; a
    timed wait's nonzero return leaves the loop with no result claim. (2)
    `pthread_cond_signal` while the waiter's mutex is held. (3)
    `pthread_cond_init`/`pthread_cond_destroy` only in a quiescent phase:
    before publication, after the last signal is ordered by the mutex (a
    derived single-signal premise), or after a checked join of every live
    thread. **These stay refused:** `pthread_cond_broadcast`; a wait outside a
    predicate loop; a signal without the mutex; one condvar used with two
    mutexes; statically initialised condvars; C11 `cnd_*` waits (the libc
    custody declarations are unchanged); rwlocks; try/timed mutex locks;
    recursive and dynamic mutex shapes. The lift is not a mutual-exclusion,
    deadlock or liveness claim, and the per-unit resource route still refuses
    every declared condvar call. `QCore.CondvarJoin`;
    [report](../reports/w47-condvar-join-publication/README.md).
